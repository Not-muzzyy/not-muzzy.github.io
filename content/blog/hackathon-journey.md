Hackathons have become an important part of my journey as a developer because they force me to do something that normal development does not always demand: **turn an idea into a working solution under extreme time constraints**.

My hackathon journey started at **RYMEC in 2025**. Later, I participated in **HackArena 2K26 at Jain College of Engineering, Hubli, powered by Unstop**. The two events were very different. My first hackathon gave me my foundation. My second showed me how much I had improved.

## 1. My First Hackathon — RYMEC 2025

My first hackathon was a **national-level hackathon at RYMEC in 2025**. I participated with a team of four members, and we had only **8 hours** to build our solution.

For a first-time hackathon participant, the environment was completely different from normal college projects. We had limited time, limited room for mistakes, and a requirement to turn an idea into something that could actually be demonstrated.

### Our Idea: An AI-Powered To-Do List

We built an **AI-powered to-do list application** designed to reduce the amount of manual task management required from users. Traditional to-do applications depend heavily on users to create, organize, update, and manage their tasks. Our idea was to make the process more intelligent by using **AI-powered automation** to manage tasks dynamically.

> **A task manager that does more than store tasks — it uses AI to help automate task management.**

This project introduced me to the practical side of integrating artificial intelligence into a software product.

### Building Under an 8-Hour Deadline

The biggest challenge was the clock. We had to move quickly from:
`Idea → Planning → Development → AI Integration → Testing → Presentation`

Every decision mattered. We could not spend hours perfecting a single feature while leaving the rest of the product unfinished. We had to identify the core functionality, divide responsibilities across the team, and focus on producing a working prototype.

That experience taught me one of my earliest lessons in software development:

> **A technically ambitious idea has little value if you cannot deliver a working implementation.**

### The Result

After eight hours of development, we presented our project. Our team finished in the **Top 10**. More than the ranking, the experience showed me what real-time product development felt like. It became the starting point for my interest in building solutions through competitive development environments.

## 2. HackArena 2K26 — Jain College of Engineering

My second major hackathon experience came at **Jain College of Engineering, Hubli**, through **HackArena 2K26, powered by Unstop**.

This time, the scale and selection process were different. The competition began with an online PPT-based idea submission round where approximately **200 teams** participated. Only **50 teams were selected** for the offline hackathon, and our team was one of them.

### 24 Hours to Build

Unlike my first 8-hour hackathon, HackArena 2K26 gave us **24 hours**. Three times the development window did not mean the challenge became easy. Instead, we had more time to think about the product, improve the implementation, test our solution, and make our presentation stronger.

The environment pushed us to think beyond simply making something that worked. We had to think about product usability, technical innovation, reliability, and real-world applicability. The longer development window allowed us to go deeper into the solution rather than only building a minimum viable prototype.

### The Top-5 Finish

After the 24-hour hackathon, our team achieved a **Top-5 position**.

This was a major milestone for me because it represented visible progress. My first experience ended with a Top-10 finish after 8 hours. My second major experience ended with a Top-5 finish after 24 hours, following a highly competitive selection process. The ranking was important, but the progression was even more valuable.

## 3. What Changed Between the Two Events?

The biggest difference was not simply the amount of time available—it was my approach.

During my first hackathon, I was learning how hackathons worked. By the time I reached HackArena 2K26, I had already experienced the pressure of rapid development, team coordination, technical decisions, debugging, and presenting a project.

My first hackathon taught me to focus on creating a working application. My second experience pushed me to think more about the complete solution. That shift matters in software engineering. A good developer should not only ask:

*“Can I build this?”*

They should also ask:

*“Why should this exist, who does it help, and how can I make it useful?”*

## 4. What Hackathons Taught Me

- **Rapid Prototyping:** Learning how to move quickly from an abstract idea to a functional prototype directly applies to AI development, web apps, and startup-style product development.
- **Problem Solving Under Pressure:** A hackathon rarely goes exactly according to plan. Bugs appear, features break, and time disappears. You have to adapt quickly and keep moving.
- **Team Collaboration:** Technical skills matter, but so do communication, task delegation, coordination, and supporting your teammates under tight deadlines.
- **Presenting Technical Work:** Building something is only part of the job. You also need to explain what problem you identified, why your solution matters, and how it could be used in the real world.
